# SEO page 1 — target: "new businesses registered in Denver"

**Title:** New Businesses Registered in Denver County — Updated Weekly
**Slug:** /new-businesses-denver-county

**H1:** How to find new businesses registered in Denver County

Denver County records hundreds of new business registrations every month, and
all of them are public. Here's where the data actually lives and how to work
with it.

**H2: The official source**
The Colorado Secretary of State maintains the registration record for every
entity formed in the state, and publishes it as open data. There's no fee and
no account required to look up an individual business.

**H2: What each record tells you**
Business name, entity type (LLC, corporation, nonprofit), formation date,
principal address, mailing address, registered agent, and current status.
What it does not include: phone numbers, email addresses, revenue, or employee
counts. No public state registry has those.

**H2: Why formation date matters more than anything else**
A business that registered eleven days ago has not chosen an accountant, an
insurance broker, a bank, or a web designer. One that registered eight months
ago has. If you sell services to small businesses, the age of the record is the
single strongest signal in the file.

**H2: The three ways to work with this data**
1. *Search one at a time.* Free, fine for checking a specific company, useless
   for building a prospect list.
2. *Pull the bulk data yourself.* The state publishes an API. Expect to handle
   paging, deduplication, address normalization, and industry tagging. Budget
   about fifteen hours to build and an hour a month to maintain.
3. *Subscribe to a filtered feed.* What we do — [link].

**H2: Rules for contacting new businesses**
Public record does not mean open season. Email outreach falls under CAN-SPAM,
calls fall under the TCPA and Do Not Call rules, and Colorado has its own
telemarketing provisions. Direct mail to a principal business address is the
least regulated channel and is why it still works here.

# SEO page 2 — target: "colorado llc filings this week"

**Title:** Colorado LLC Filings This Week — What's Public and How to Get It
**Slug:** /colorado-llc-filings-this-week

**H1:** Colorado LLC filings this week

**H2: How quickly filings become public**
Colorado processes electronic filings quickly, and records appear in the public
database shortly after. In practice you can see companies that formed within
the last several days.

**H2: How many LLCs form in Colorado**
Colorado's registry holds over a million entity records going back to the
1800s. New formations run in the thousands per month statewide, concentrated in
the Front Range corridor — Denver, Arapahoe, Jefferson, El Paso, Boulder, Weld,
and Larimer counties.

**H2: Reading an entity type code**
`DLLC` domestic LLC, formed in Colorado. `FLLC` foreign LLC, formed elsewhere
and registered to operate here. `DPC` domestic profit corporation. `DNC`
domestic nonprofit. Foreign registrations are a different sales motion — that
company already has vendors somewhere else.

**H2: Filtering to what's useful**
An unfiltered weekly file is thousands of rows across every industry in every
county. Nobody works that list. The usable slice is one county and one
category — typically thirty to sixty records a week, which is a manageable
week of outreach.

# SEO page 3 — target: "how to find newly formed businesses"

**Title:** How to Find Newly Formed Businesses (Without Buying a Junk List)
**Slug:** /how-to-find-newly-formed-businesses

**H1:** How to find newly formed businesses

**H2: Start with the state, not a data broker**
Every US state maintains a registry of business formations. Some publish it as
free open data — Colorado, New York, Pennsylvania, Oregon, and Connecticut
among them. Others charge for bulk access or prohibit automated access to their
search portal entirely. North Carolina, for example, prohibits scripted
searching and sells a bulk contract for a substantial annual fee. Check the
terms of any source before you automate against it.

**H2: Why most purchased lead lists disappoint**
Three recurring problems. The list is national when your service area is one
metro. The "verified" email addresses were pattern-guessed against domains that
don't exist yet, so they bounce. And the same list has been sold to everyone
else who sells what you sell.

**H2: What a good new-business list actually has**
Recent formation dates, a tight geography, an industry filter, a real physical
address, and no duplicates. That's it. Anything promising more on a
nine-day-old company is overstating what exists.

**H2: The timing window**
Most vendor decisions get made in a new business's first several months. That's
the whole thesis for working formation data: not that these companies are
better prospects, but that they haven't chosen yet.

## SEO notes

- Publish all three on your own domain, one per week. Don't publish them all at
  once — a brand-new site dropping three optimized pages on day one looks
  exactly like what it is.
- Internal-link each page to the other two and to the landing page.
- These are low-competition, high-intent queries. Expect meaningful traffic in
  three to six months, not three weeks. This channel is the slow compounding
  half of the plan; the community posts are the fast half.
- Rewrite these in your own voice before publishing. Google doesn't penalize AI
  assistance, but pages that read like everyone else's don't earn links.
